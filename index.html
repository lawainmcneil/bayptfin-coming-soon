The problem retirement creates

Saving and spending are different problems

For thirty years the goal was simple: put more in. Retirement flips it. Now the question is how much you can take out, from which account, in what order, without running short or leaving money on the table.

That means decisions about when to claim Social Security, how to handle taxes on withdrawals, what to do about health insurance before Medicare, and how your income could hold up if markets fall early in retirement. Those decisions connect. We help you make them together instead of one at a time.

Why we start with you, not products

The plan you’ll stick with matters as much as the plan that looks best on paper

Two people with the same savings can want very different retirements. One sleeps best knowing a set amount arrives every month. The other would rather keep control and stay invested. Neither is wrong.

RISA®, Retirement Income Style Awareness, is a short assessment that shows you which one you are. Developed by retirement researchers, it looks at two things: how you feel about safety versus growth, and whether you’d rather commit to a plan or keep your options open. Your answers point to one of four income styles, each with its own way of turning savings into income.
